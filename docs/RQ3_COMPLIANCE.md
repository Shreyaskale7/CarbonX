# RQ3 as a compliance outcome: what each reading shows with no physical change

_Generated by `scripts/rq3_compliance.py` from `data/cea/factors.csv`. Do not edit by hand._

Take an entity whose fuel use, electricity use and production are **identical** in the baseline year and in FY2025-26. Its emission intensity (GEI) still moves, because the grid factor applied to its electricity differs between the two years. If electricity is a share *s* of its baseline-year emissions in the target basis, the change is exactly *s* × (*f*ₐ / *f*_b − 1). This file evaluates that for the three readings of BEE's rule found in `docs/RQ3_FINDINGS.md`.

**Assumed, not verified.** (1) The baseline year is FY2023-24; the last table repeats the calculation for neighbouring years. (2) The baseline GEI used that year's factor as first published. Both must be checked against the target notifications. Factor series: `grid.weighted_avg_incl_re_captive.incl_imports`. No plant data is used; this is a property of the published factors, stated per Scope 2 share. It is not a claim about any entity's compliance.

## Reading it

- **The three readings give three different outcomes for the same plant.** Under the consistency reading the factor contributes nothing, and the whole target must come from physical change. Under the other two, part or all of the target is met by the fall in the grid factor.
- **The size of that part depends on the Scope 2 share**, which is why this is a table and not one number. For an entity with little purchased electricity the readings barely differ.
- **The direction is an accident of the years involved.** The grid factor fell between these years. In a year where it rises, the same readings would make targets harder to meet, by the same arithmetic.
- **What this does not show:** whether targets were set expecting the grid factor to fall, or which reading verifiers actually applied. Both are questions for BEE and for the first cycle's verification statements.
