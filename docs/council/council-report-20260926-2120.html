Where the council agrees

  • The biggest risk is the team, not the code. One person and an AI assistant built everything, and TEAM.md is blank. RVCE vivas grade each member separately. Each member must own one component and be able to defend it.
  • RQ3 is the real finding and the story of the project. The first FY2025-26 factor appeared the day after the deadline. Not having read the BEE procedure is now the most expensive gap.
  • The AI/ML component is empty and planned the wrong way. Zero bills, splits by DISCOM that need many DISCOMs, circular learning-to-rank labels, and no uncertainty data. All five advisors cut learning-to-rank.
  • Cut the Next.js workbench. Four of five advisors said so.
  • Cheap fixes: push the repo so CI runs, and label the 7.8% figure as synthetic.

Where the council clashes

What the AI/ML component should be

The Contrarian, Outsider and Executor would keep bill extraction, shrunk to a calibrated extractor with a synthetic fallback. The First Principles Thinker counters that CCTS plants report electricity from HT meters and SCADA, not scanned bills, and would forecast the next CEA factor instead.

Ruling: rubric fit wins. Build a small, honest, calibrated extractor. The forecast is optional, and reviewers warned it risks predicting a figure from the data used to compute it.

The lifecycle automaton

The Expansionist and Outsider would cut it. The Executor and Contrarian would keep a small one with model-checked safety properties, because the type checker alone is textbook material.

Ruling: keep it: 5 states, model-checked. It carries the ToC grade.

Expand or contract

The Expansionist pushed BRSR filings, CBAM and a policy paper. All five reviewers rejected this: BRSR is company-level, not plant-level GEI, and adding it breaks the project's own scope rule.

Ruling: contract.

Blind spots the council caught

  • The headline could collapse. If BEE prescribes a fixed factor version, the one-day gap is moot. Write the fallback framing before reading, archive the v22 evidence, and check the gazette date.
  • The 5.1–7.8% swing is in tCO2e, not in compliance. With GEI null, "policy flips compliance" is not shown.
  • FY2023-24 may be the CCTS baseline year. Its methodology break may be the finding, not noise.
  • The app connects as superuser, so invariant 3 isn't actually enforced by the database.
  • AI-assistance disclosure under RVCE's academic-integrity rules.
  • Nobody has the three course rubrics. Get them, plus faculty-guide sign-off, before cutting.

The recommendation

Stop adding breadth. Turn one finding and one guarantee into something every member can defend.

  1. Week 1: assign ownership; disclose AI use; push with CI green; move the app to a non-owner database role; read BEE and archive the v22 evidence.
  2. Weeks 2–4: denominator versioning, so GEI is computed. Recompute RQ3 as a GEI and compliance delta on a cited sector profile.
  3. Weeks 3–6: RQ1 per-sector curve from cited Scope 1/2 splits.
  4. Weeks 2–8: collect bills now. Build a pdfplumber extractor with calibrated confidence. If fewer than about 30 bills arrive by week 4, go synthetic and say so.
  5. Weeks 5–8: 5-state credit automaton with model-checked safety properties.
  6. Weeks 9–13: Zenodo DOI, before/after reproducibility demo, cross-defence viva rehearsals.

Cut learning-to-rank, the Next.js workbench, grid-factor uncertainty, arXiv, BRSR and CBAM, with whole-team written sign-off.

The one thing to do first

This week, read BEE's Detailed Procedure for Compliance Mechanism under CCTS v1.0 and record in docs/RQ3_FINDINGS.md which factor version it prescribes, if any, with the v22 evidence archived alongside. Every other priority depends on whether RQ3 survives.
