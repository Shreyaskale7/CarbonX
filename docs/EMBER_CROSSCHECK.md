# Ember cross-check: an independent estimate of India's grid intensity

_Generated by `scripts/ember_crosscheck.py` from `data/ember/india_monthly_national.csv` and `data/cea/factors.csv`. Do not edit by hand._

Ember publishes monthly generation and power-sector emissions for India (CC BY 4.0; data to 2025-11). Dividing one by the other over a financial year gives a national carbon intensity that does not come from CEA's database. This file asks two things: do the two agree, and does Ember's monthly data say anything useful about a year CEA has not published yet?

**Read this first.** (1) Ember and CEA do not measure the same thing: Ember divides all power-sector emissions by all generation; CEA's series are weighted averages of station emission rates under its own definitions. A constant gap between them is expected and is not an error in either. (2) Ember itself draws on Indian official generation statistics, so it is an independent *calculation*, not independent *measurement*. (3) The file used is Ember's current one, not what Ember had published at each past deadline, so the backtest below is more favourable to Ember than a real one would have been. (4) The Scope 2 qualifier applies to every percentage here.

## What this does and does not show

- The cases are few and overlap (two CEA series, the same years), so this cannot establish that the indicator works. It can show whether it is worth pursuing.
- If it helps, the practical meaning is for RQ3: by the reporting deadline an entity could know roughly where the unpublished factor will land, from public data.
- If it does not help, that is also informative: the two sources define intensity differently enough that one does not track the other year to year.
- A proper test needs Ember's archived monthly releases (what was actually public at each deadline). We have only the current file.
