What the registry shows

Three questions about India's grid emission factor, answered from CEA's own workbooks and BEE's compliance procedure.

Provisional. Everything that quotes BEE rests on one PDF whose internal date (8 November 2023) is earlier than the reported notification. It must be checked against the notified text before any of this is cited.

Which factor version applies?

BEE's procedure gives a rule for purchased electricity:

“the latest average grid emission factors from CO2 baseline database for Indian Power Sector published by the Central Electricity Authority”
Detailed Procedure for Compliance Mechanism under CCTS, p. 11, clause (vii)

The rule names a source. It leaves two things open: when "latest" is judged, and which of CEA's average series is meant. Both change the number.

The same plant, three outcomes

Take a plant that changes nothing between the baseline year and FY2025-26. Its emission intensity still moves, because the grid factor applied to its electricity differs. The change is the Scope 2 share of its target basis times the change in the factor.

How large are year-on-year changes?

In the longest all-India series, 18 of 25 yearly changes were larger than 1%, and 4 were larger than 3%. The FY2025-26 targets ask for a 1 to 3% reduction in emission intensity.

The comparison holds in full only for an entity whose target basis is entirely purchased electricity. For everyone else the effect scales with the Scope 2 share, which is the next chart.

Can the missing factor be predicted?

When reports are due, the year's factor is not yet published, so "the latest" is last year's. Over 20 years that guess missed by 1.99% on average, and by 5.7% at worst. Four simple forecasting models were tested against it. None did better.

Each forecast used only what CEA had published before that year first appeared. A band built from earlier misses contained 10 of 12 later values at 80% and 11 of 12 at 90%. For the series CarbonX uses, the FY2025-26 value fell outside the 90% band.

When does a factor change alone become material?

GHG Protocol requires base-year recalculation when emission factors are updated, and SBTi treats a 5% change as significant. A factor change of d moves the total by the Scope 2 share times d.

CEA restating a past year

At most 0.78%. It does not reach 5% at any share.

The reading of "latest"

4.87%. It stays just under 5% even at a 100% share.

The choice of series

21.85%. It crosses 5% once electricity is more than 23% of the target basis.

Placing each CCTS sector on this curve needs a cited Scope 2 share of the 2024-27 target basis. Those citations have not been collected yet.
