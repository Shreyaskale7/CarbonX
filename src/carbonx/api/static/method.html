How it works

Three ideas carry the system: two clocks on every row, a calculation language that checks units, and a credit lifecycle small enough to prove things about.

Two clocks on every row

Each factor, activity and calculation carries the period it describes and the moment the system learned it. A correction closes the old row and opens a new one. Nothing is overwritten.

A report records the moment it was read. Regenerating it reads the ledger as of that moment, so later revisions and backdated corrections cannot reach it.

Three properties are proved for this automaton and checked by an explicit-state model checker: a surrendered or retired credit never moves again, a live credit has exactly one holder, and the number of credits is conserved.

Where the guarantees stop

The audit trail and the no-overwrite rules hold against the application, which connects as a role that cannot disable them. They do not hold against the database owner or a superuser, who must be trusted.

The lifecycle proofs are written by hand and checked by enumeration on small instances. They are not machine-verified. The type system has eight documented escape hatches.
